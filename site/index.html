The idea

AI changes tasks before it changes job titles.

A job is a bundle of tasks. AI does not delete jobs overnight. It eats some tasks inside them, and what is left over becomes a different job. Oroboros shows a person that process, on their own work.

  1. 01

    Consume

    We list your real tasks and show which ones AI is likely to absorb, using live public signals.

  2. 02

    Regrow

    We match what is left to real occupations, or a blend of two, and show the gap to each.

  3. 03

    Plan

    You get an ordered learning plan with free courses, hours per step and a proof task for each.

How it works

Three formulas. Nothing hidden.

Every number in the app is computed by code from public data. The same three formulas are printed next to every result.

A · Momentum mc = clamp( last 90 days ÷ past-year average, 0.8, 1.2 )

For each of ten AI capability areas we compare the last 90 days of arXiv papers, GitHub commits on reference projects and Hacker News hiring posts with that area's own past year. The cap keeps a noisy feed from taking over.

Modelling assumption, refreshed nightly.

B · Absorption At = min( 1, Et × mc × s )

Et is the task's exposure from published datasets. We keep the lowest and the highest estimate, so when datasets disagree you see a range, not a fake single number. s is the scenario: Slow 0.6, Gradual 1.0, Shockwave 1.6.

Consumed at A ≥ 0.6 · shrinking 0.35 to 0.6 · safe below 0.35. If the range crosses a line, the task is marked uncertain.

C · Ranking Rank = Fit × Resilience

Fit is how much of a role's core work you already do. Resilience is how much of that role survives the same scenario, so we never send you to a job that is being eaten too.

Composite roles are always two real, named occupations. We never coin a title.

                Sources

                Every number has a date.

                Layers that change by release show a version. The live layer shows when it was fetched. If a feed fails, the site keeps the last good snapshot and says so.

                Known limits, said up front

                • O*NET is US-based. It is the most detailed open task database, but it describes US jobs. Malaysian job titles map to it through ISCO-08 codes, the base of MASCO. Treat the match as close, not exact.
                • Exposure is not job loss. The datasets say which tasks an AI system could speed up, not which jobs disappear.
                • Exposure data update by release, not daily. Only the momentum layer updates nightly.
                • Robots are under-counted. The exposure datasets measure language-model AI. Physical work can look safer here than it is.
                • Momentum is our assumption. The 0.8 to 1.2 cap and the scenario speeds are stated choices, printed on every screen.
                • The jobs feed is young. It joins the momentum score after 90 days of nightly history.

                Fair questions.

                Will AI take my job?

                We do not predict that, and nobody can honestly. We show which tasks inside your job are exposed, under three stated assumptions, and what you can build from the rest.

                Where do the numbers come from?

                Tasks come from O*NET 31.0. Exposure comes from three published datasets, kept separate so their disagreement shows as a range. Momentum comes from arXiv, Hacker News, GitHub and job boards, refreshed every night. Every chart says where and when.

                Why US data for a Malaysian worker?

                O*NET is the most detailed open task database there is, and it is US-based. Malaysian job titles map to it through the ISCO occupation codes that MASCO also uses. We flag this on every screen instead of hiding it.

                Is anything I type stored?

                No. The engine runs in your browser. The demo uses five synthetic people. Please do not paste a real CV with personal details.

                Which AI model is used?

                The core uses no language model at all. Two optional jobs, reading pasted CV text and writing plain-language summaries, use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Every role, task and course it mentions is checked against our database first, or dropped.

                Can a company use this to decide who to let go?

                No. Oroboros is for the person doing the job. It does not rate people and must not be used for hiring or firing decisions.
